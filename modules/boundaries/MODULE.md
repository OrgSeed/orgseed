# Module: boundaries

**For.** Deciding what the organization keeps, what never leaves it, which outside channels may receive what, and how the use of AI in its work is disclosed.

**Adds.** `org/RECORDS_POLICY.md` (format below), written in the founding session from the founder's answers.

**Works with.** `tool-making` (a tier-3 tool, one that uses the network, needs a channel check), `membership` (guests), `claim-discipline` (sources saved under their terms).

**Costs.** A short step at founding; a check before anything is sent anywhere.

## RECORDS_POLICY.md format

```
# Records policy
Data by default: only public or synthetic data, unless the founder decides on more
Never leaves this folder: (founder's list: confidential material, other people's personal data, keys)
May leave only with the founder's decision: (e.g. drafts, records)
Outside channels approved: (each: service, what may be sent, terms checked on date, copy at sources/...)
Disclosure of AI involvement: (the founder's policy for each audience)
Retention: what is kept, and for how long
adopted_by: D-....
```

## Rules

### BND-1 The founder's never-leave list is absolute
rule: Nothing on the never-leave list goes into any outside channel, any tool input that leaves the machine, any example or any public topic, without a new decision by the founder.
why: confidential material can leak through examples and convenience as easily as through deliberate disclosure [opinion].

### BND-2 Check the channel before sending
rule: Before any material goes to an outside AI service, API or website, check that the channel is approved for that kind of material in org/RECORDS_POLICY.md. If it is not listed, ask.
why: outside services keep and use what they receive under their own terms, which differ by service and by plan.

### BND-3 Terms are read, saved and dated
rule: When the organization relies on an outside service, read its terms on what it does with inputs and what it permits users to do, save a dated copy in sources/, and re-check when the terms change.
why: a channel that was safe when chosen can change; the dated copy shows what the organization relied on and when.

### BND-4 No other people's personal data
rule: Do not collect or store personal data about people other than the founder, unless the founder decides it is needed and lawful for the goal. Never infer sensitive traits (such as gender) from names.
why: people who are not members have not consented to being recorded by this organization.

### BND-5 Outside knowledge comes in as patterns, not artifacts
rule: When the founder brings in knowledge from elsewhere (another job, another project), record it as general patterns that could be about any organization. Do not store documents or details that belong to that other place.
why: knowledge the founder carries is theirs to use; material that belongs to another organization is not.

### BND-6 Disclose AI involvement as the founder decides
rule: The records policy states how the organization discloses AI involvement in its work, for each audience. Record which member contributed what, so any disclosure can be accurate.
why: readers increasingly ask how work was produced; an accurate record makes an honest answer possible.

### BND-7 The founder answers for what leaves
rule: Members draft; the founder sends, publishes, files and signs.
why: responsibility for an organization's outward acts sits with people who can be held to account for them.

## Grounding

This module is here so that the organization decides what it keeps, what never leaves it, which outside channels may receive what, and how it discloses the use of AI in its work, because outside services keep and use what they receive under their own terms, and people who are not members have not consented to being recorded by it. No theory is claimed for it, because none has been read for it. It is therefore a design guess, which a scholar may theorize and test, for example by running organizations with it on and with it off and comparing their records. Its `lit:` lines, where it has any, are leads unless they say read (`docs/LITERATURE.md`).
